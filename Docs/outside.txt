MY BIG ADVENTURE

For a 6 month period (July 8th, 2004 to Jan 9th, 2005) I laid rubber to the road and experienced a whirl-wind tour of the US, starting from and ending in Portland, OR.  Traveling in a converted Ford E350 Extended Van, my trip took me in a clockwise direction around the country.  In my travels I passed through 35 states and in most states I experienced at least one activity, and in many states I took part in multiple activities, all depending on how the state's offerings mated to my interests.

Normally this kind of travel adventure is somewhat commonplace and perhaps even humdrum to folks who are hardcode outdoors/travel people, but in my case my travels have been extensively chronicalled on a website, http://www.mybigadventure.com.  The website is the twist because I'm not sure if anyone has created a home-grown website as extensive as mine until now.  The site is crammed packed with pictures (50,000) and write-ups that cover everything from biking (15) to hiking (10) to skiing (1) to snowshoeing (1) to sight-seeing (70).  Along the way I've chronacled my meetings with family (9), friends (21), and acquaintenances (25) I've met on the road.  Additionally, any oddities (20), mishaps (17) and revelations (10) encountered on the trip have been written up as well.

Coursing through the veins of the website you'll find a humorous undercurrent.  Where possible I have tried to make light of the mundane and at times even poke fun at myself.  Some of the content is spoofed, all in the name of fun.  Navigation-wise, the website is very "inter-connected", meaning it is very easy to get from one place to another and has a minimual learning curve.

I'm not a travel writer by trade, rather, I'm an independent software consultant of all things.  However, since creating this website I've been told time and time again that I should become a writer.  Well, that day may or may not come to pass.  All I know is the thought of getting something published shoulds like an entertaining, fun process.  If my article and website can inspire someone to get out there and travel, then I will have considered it to have been a success.

Here are some short clips of what I could write for your folks regarding my trip.  FYI I can taylor my website to make things easier for your readers. I'm open to any and all suggestions in helping mate the website to the article.

CLIP #1 - LIVING ON THE ROAD IN A 11x5x4 METAL BOX
--------------------------------------------------

While traveling in style has never been a requirement of mine, riding rough-shot on the back of uncomfort has always been just as undesireable.  As part of my pre-trip planning in preparation for my cross-country road trip around the U.S., I had to find a home on wheels.  Something that was practical, economical, reliable, and of all things, stealth-like in appearance.

My search took me high and low, from pre-converted vans to vans that needed to be converted.  From dealerships to for-sale-by-owner to finally eBay of all places.  Yes, that's right, eBay.  I bought my van on eBay.  In the past I had heard of such things, of folks buying vehicles on the Web, but never did I think I'd bid on anything so expensive on the Internet.  But lady-luck smiled upon me and in the end my bid was the highest and a 1999 Ford E350 Extended Passenger Van was all mine, and at $4,000 under retail book value no less.

The van was a gem, fleet-maintained vehicle owned by the State of Oregon, operated by a community college in the high desert in Eastern Oregon.  Other than some sand in the engine comparment, I couldn't find anything wrong with the vehicle, and post-sale maintenance proved the same, it was a great buy.

To take my van from passenger hauler to just hauling a single passenger (me) around the country with the minimual of living basics, I needed to devise a game plan.  First task was to build the Van Conversion Team.  Not being the kind of person to go it alone when other folks are around and willing to help out, I enlisted my father, Richard and mother, Betty.

Richard was deemed "The Hammer" because he could always be found hammering something, drawing on his past home building experience.  Betty was deemed "The Needle" for all the sowing she had done in the past when I was a kid.  while a little rusty at this stage of the game, she would wipe away the cobwebs from the sowing machine and lend a hand at creating the various curtains that the van conversion project would require.

The van conversion took 3 1/2 weeks of part-time work.  In the end we designed and built an RV that offered cold running water, 76" long bed, a multitude of storage, a fold-down table, a dual burner propane stove, a safe, carpeting all around, curtains, an AC converter, and 2 RV backup batteries.  As 11x5x4 metal boxes go, my metal box on wheels wasn't half-bad.

With the van conversion completed, the most challenging part of my adventure was just about to begin, the dailyshoehorning of my 6'4" body into and out of my 11x5x4 metal box on wheels.  Being a tall person, I appreciate the little things in life like bathroom mirrors that don't cut off my head when I'm standing tall in their presence, like kitchen countertops that don't require me to bend over at uncomfortable angles because they are height-challenged, like tables that are high enough where I don't have to worry about jambing my knees when I pull my chair up to them.  But in my world of the road, all such amenities were thrown out the window.  It was time to march to a different beat, to contort and conform my body into the pretzel-like positions long since learned from taking Yoga for so many years.
