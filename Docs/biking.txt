 Located in the Norbeck Wildlife Preserve, just south of Mount Rushmore National Monument in the Black Hills. The Iron Mountain Trail loop forms the core of a 15 mile system on the southeast corner of the Norbeck Wilderness Preserve. This trail system is a combination of wide single-track trails and old forest access roads of generally moderate difficulty. Steady climbs and descents, rocky spots, dozens of cliff and rock formations, and a continuous forest cover of pine and aspen make this area about as close to mountain bike heaven as you can get. From a ridge on the start of the loop, you can get a great view across to the carvings on Mount Rushmore, two miles north.

Iron Mountain Road is another incredibly beautiful highway in the Custer State Park area.  Also consisting of tight turns and some tough climbs, this highway has small tunnels similar to that on the Needles Highway, which can provide as incredible view of Mount Rushmore to the North.  It also has pigtail bridges that twist and turn through the hills. 

Taking you from Highway 16 near the Mount Rushmore area in Keystone, to Custer and the Custer State Park, this highway is very narrow due to the terrain of the area and cycling here can be dangerous with the large amount of traffic this highway gets daily throughout the Summer.  Tight turns will require drivers to travel slowly, but because there is so much to see, drivers can easily get distracted.  You will definitely need to keep your eyes open.  Tall vehicles and on-the-top bike racks are unable to enter this highway because of the small tunnels and the bridges. 

There are also possibilities of encountering buffalo, deer and elk on the roadway in the Custer State Park area.  If you do encounter buffalo or elk, be sure to keep your distance, as these animals are VERY DANGEROUS and can charge without warning.  Deerfield Lake is 14 miles northwest of Hill City in the center of the Black Hills National Forest. The Deerfield Lake Loop Trail (40L) provides a ten-mile loop route of generally moderate difficulty through a classic Black Hills area of pine and aspen forest, grassy meadows, and some challenging hillsides on a mixture of single- and double-track trails. Rocks, sharp turns, occasional logs, and eroded spots add to the technical nature of the route. At Deerfield Lake you will be in the center of the Black Hills Forest, away from the main flow of the tourist circuit farther east. Anywhere from several hours to the better part of a day can be spent in this area, and combined with the 18-mile Deerfield Trail (40) that connects this loop with the Centennial Trail to the east, the riding possibilities become limitless.

I actually rode this trail twice starting both times at Dutchman Campground. Once in each direction. Both ways were fun. In my opinion, riding counter-clockwise is tougher as the longer climbs are encountered at the end of the ride. The trail is 80%-90% singletrack with some jeep roads interspersed. Lots of ups and downs. Some steep, but mostly rideable. One or two longer climbs/descents. The trail runs through open ponderosa pine forest and prairie around the lake and provides excellent views of the lake and the surrounding hills. The trail is not terribly technical although there are some deeply rutted sections and others that are loose and rocky.
