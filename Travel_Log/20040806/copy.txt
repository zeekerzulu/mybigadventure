My goal for the day was to see all of the Park's remaining major sights.  Up to this point, I had seen only 2 of the 7 

sights, Mammoth Hot Springs and Norris Geyser Basin.  Those that remained included 1) Fountain Paint Pot Area, 2) Old 

Faithful Area, 3) West Thumb Geyser Basin, 4) Mud Volcano, 5) Canyon Area.  It was an aggressive plan so I took great care in 

plotting my route to eliminate all unnecessary driving.

road dust settled, I ran out of daylight and wasn't able to see the Canyon Area, that would have to wait until tomorrow.  The 

dogleg up and out of the Park to Gardiner, MT was taken to avoid paying the $14 campsite fee.  It wasn't that far to drive 

and I had to be up in that part of the Park anyway for the two hikes I was planning to take the next day.

I found myself all pooped out when I finished the trail.  I had seen 4 of the day's 5 planned sights which wasn't bad 

considering doing more than 3 is a lot.  I wonder what the average is?  With family in tow, its probably only 2.  The nice 

thing about traveling solo is you don't get bogged down by the collective interests of the group.  I spend time on what I'm 

interested in when touring a sight and whisk by the rest.
<p>
I decided to end the sight-seeing for the day and travel out of the Park back to Gardiner, MT, located on the front doorstep 

of the Park's North Entrance.  I didn't feel like paying $14 to sleep in my van again, plus my hikes the next day were in the 

Mammoth Hot Springs Area which is a stone's throw from the North Entrance.
<p>
Upon arrival at Gardiner, MT I found it difficult to find a suitable car-camping spot in town.  Most of the side-roads in 

town were dirt roads and didn't look very inviting.  So I decided to hide-in-plain-site and park right on the main 

thoroughfare.
<p>
I fixed dinner and retired early.  Tomorrow I'd have a busy day of hiking two trails (Sepulcher Mountain and Bunsen Peak) and 

sight-seeing the 7th and last Yellowstone sight, the Canyon Area.
<p>
When laying my head down to rest, little did I know my sleep would be interrupted in the wee hours of the morning by a 

visitor who was both welcome and unwelcome.
